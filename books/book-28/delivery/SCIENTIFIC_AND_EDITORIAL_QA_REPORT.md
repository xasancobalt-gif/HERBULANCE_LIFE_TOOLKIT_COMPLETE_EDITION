# Scientific & Editorial QA Report

**Product:** HERBULANCE Book 28 — Ear Health, Hearing & Balance  
**Edition:** 1.0, English, 2026

## Scope of this report

This report records the editorial and safety checks performed during development of the digital edition. It is not a clinical certification. **No named physician, audiologist, ENT specialist or other qualified clinician has individually reviewed or endorsed this edition.** Qualified clinical review is required before the publication is represented as patient education or used in a clinical pathway.

## Editorial completeness

- Front matter includes title, brand context, educational-use notice, medical disclaimer, usage guidance, intended audience, learning outcomes, contents, learning roadmap and urgent-warning guide.
- All 22 required chapters are present across five parts.
- Each chapter contains learning objectives, explanatory sections, at least one applied learning element, key takeaways and two knowledge-check questions with option-level feedback.
- Practical tools include 13 printable worksheets plus visual learning sheets, a hearing-process sequence, vestibular exploration, an audiogram activity, glossary and FAQ.
- Cross-promotional references identify companion volumes as part of the wider toolkit roadmap and do not fabricate product URLs.
- Language is written for a general audience and avoids assuming one country's health system.

## Medical accuracy and safety review

The following safety positions were checked for internal consistency throughout the manuscript:

- Sudden hearing loss is consistently described as requiring urgent assessment and not a "wait and see" symptom.
- Vertigo with neurological symptoms is consistently described as an emergency, with instructions not to self-treat or drive.
- Serious infection signs, post-traumatic clear fluid, facial weakness, major ear trauma and suspected button batteries are flagged for urgent care.
- Cotton buds, instruments, fingers and other objects are not recommended for the ear canal.
- Ear candling is explicitly not recommended.
- Oils, alcohol, peroxide, vinegar and herbal substances are presented as unsuitable for indiscriminate home use, especially with possible perforation, tubes, infection or previous surgery.
- Home syringing is not presented as a universal treatment.
- BPPV repositioning is described as condition-specific and requiring appropriate assessment; readers are not taught to self-diagnose vestibular conditions.
- Tinnitus is not described as universally curable; no supplement, diet or sound therapy is claimed to work for everyone.
- Hearing aids are not described as restoring normal hearing.
- Cognitive-health associations are distinguished from established causation.
- Deaf culture, sign languages and multimodal communication are treated respectfully.
- Access and screening variation across countries is acknowledged.

## Evidence handling

- References prioritise WHO, NIDCD/NIH, NHS, CDC/NIOSH, AAO-HNS and Cochrane sources, with other professional/patient resources where relevant.
- The Evidence Centre distinguishes established guidance, emerging research and unsupported or uncertain claims.
- The book states that it is not a systematic review and that public-health guidance cannot determine individual diagnosis.
- References were selected for topical relevance. URLs were checked at development time; external pages can change after publication.
- No invented study, author, DOI or quotation was intentionally included. Any citation correction identified in future review should be logged and corrected in a subsequent edition.

## Known editorial limitations

The following areas require independent specialist review if this edition is to be used in a clinical or patient-education context:

1. Emergency triage wording and alignment with local urgent-care pathways.
2. Paediatric ear infection, hearing screening and language-access content.
3. Vestibular diagnosis, emergency differentiation and rehabilitation guidance.
4. Device candidacy, outcomes, costs and funding across health systems.
5. Medicine-related ototoxicity and balance effects.
6. Assistive technology availability in low-resource settings.
7. Local variation in wax management and audiology referral.

## Editorial standard

The edition aims to be plain-language, non-fear-based, non-commercial and inclusive. It does not promise to cure hearing loss, reverse tinnitus, eliminate vertigo or prevent every ear disorder. It encourages appropriate professional care and recognises that healthcare access is unequal.
