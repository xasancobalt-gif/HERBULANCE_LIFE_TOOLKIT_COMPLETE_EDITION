# HERBULANCE Scientific and Safety Audit

**Product:** HERBULANCE LIFE TOOLKIT — The Complete Digital Edition  
**Edition:** 1.0.0  
**Audit date:** 15 November 2026  
**Status:** Completed risk screen with unresolved professional-review limitations disclosed below

## Executive conclusion

The 30 owner-approved source editions were screened as a fixed educational corpus: **1,541 canonical chapters**, **3,969 searchable records**, and **654 consolidated structured reference records**. The source material repeatedly uses evidence, professional-care, medication, pregnancy and emergency language, and contextual samples of words such as “cure”, “guarantee” and “detox” were generally cautionary or myth-correcting rather than promotional.

This audit is **not clinical validation, medical certification, or a substitute for line-by-line review by qualified clinicians, pharmacists, registered dietitians, emergency-care educators and jurisdiction-specific legal reviewers**. No such named professional sign-off was supplied. The package therefore describes the content as general education and includes a prominent medical disclaimer. The rights holder should commission professional review before making claims that the collection is medically validated or clinically approved.

## Scope and method

1. Verified the source-aware canonical inventory for all 30 volumes and 1,541 chapters.
2. Screened canonical chapter text for professional-care, emergency, medication, pregnancy/lactation, numeric dose/threshold, evidence, cure, guarantee and detox language.
3. Inventoried 654 structured reference records and their hosts.
4. Manually sampled contexts around cure, guarantee, detox, reversal and numeric-dose/threshold hits.
5. Preserved the winning manuscripts. No health recommendation, dose, warning, statistic or claim was silently rewritten during integration.

Flag counts below indicate **review workload, not error counts**. A “dose or threshold” flag can be a nutrition label example, a diagnostic cut-off, a contaminant threshold or a medicine-related number.

## Reference profile

The most frequent structured reference hosts include WHO (84 records), NHS (69), DOI links (58), NIDDK/NIH (44), CDC (32), NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (23), PubMed (20), National Eye Institute (18), NINDS (17), MedlinePlus (17), NICE (16), NCCIH (13) and FAO (13). This is a positive traceability signal, but host authority alone does not prove that every interpretation is current or correct.

Volumes **01, 05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 15, 17, 18, 23, 27, 29, 30** yielded no records in the consolidated *structured-reference* extractor. This does not establish that those books lack sources: several use embedded resource lists, prose citations, standalone bibliographies or formats not represented by the unified schema. Their reference presentation should receive manual editorial review before claims of complete citation coverage.

## Contextual findings

- Sampled “detox” language in Volumes 01–03 rejects unsupported detox products or labels an activity as not a detox programme.
- Sampled “cure” and “guarantee” language in Volumes 02–04 cautions against cure claims, guaranteed outcomes, purity assumptions and replacing medical care.
- Sampled “reverse” language was frequently non-medical (for example, reversing a calculation) or explicitly warned that reheating/freezing cannot reverse unsafe food handling.
- Numeric flags are especially concentrated in Volume 10, where nutrient amounts and reference values are central. These numbers warrant pharmacist/dietitian review for currency, units, life-stage applicability and regional differences.
- Emergency language is especially concentrated in Volumes 09, 18, 19, 25, 28, 29 and 30. Emergency instructions should be reviewed against the buyer's country, current resuscitation guidance and local emergency numbers.
- Medication, pregnancy and contraindication language is widespread in system-health volumes. Users are repeatedly directed toward professional care, but automated detection cannot prove that every necessary caveat appears at every relevant recommendation.

## Unresolved concerns and required disclosures

1. **No independent clinical sign-off.** The integration process did not create or obtain a named clinician's approval of every chapter.
2. **Time-sensitive guidance.** Emergency, resuscitation, food-safety, nutrient, screening, medication and public-health recommendations can change. Establish a dated professional review cycle.
3. **Jurisdictional variation.** Emergency numbers, food labels, allergen rules, supplement regulation, units and clinical pathways vary by country. The books contain global examples but cannot replace local official guidance.
4. **External reference drift.** Reference links are preserved and searchable, but linked pages can move or change and generally require internet access when clicked.
5. **Search decontextualisation.** Global search returns snippets. Readers must open the full chapter, read warnings and references, and not act on a snippet alone.
6. **Individual contraindications.** Pregnancy, childhood, older age, disability, allergy, chronic disease, surgery, medicines and supplements can change what is safe.
7. **Emergency use.** The toolkit is not an emergency-response service. In an emergency, users must call their local emergency number and follow trained-dispatcher instructions rather than spend time searching the books.
8. **Reference-schema gaps.** The consolidated 654-record reference centre is a convenience index, not proof that every source citation in every book was parsed.

## Integration content-change statement

No substantive medical or scientific content change was made. Release corrections were technical: asset-path restoration, local/offline dependency replacement, syntax repair in supplied Book 08 and Book 30 scripts, shared navigation/progress controls, accessibility rendering corrections, and responsive layout corrections. Original downloads remain separately preserved and checksum-verified.

## Release recommendation

The package is technically suitable for offline educational distribution with its disclaimer and audit disclosures. Do **not** market it as medical advice, a diagnostic or treatment product, an emergency service, clinically validated, or guaranteed to improve health. Before large-scale commercial release, obtain jurisdiction-appropriate legal review and prioritized professional review of emergency, allergy, nutrient/dose, medication, pregnancy and disease-management content.
